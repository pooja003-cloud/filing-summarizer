# Top 5 risks: HP INC (HPQ)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2025-10-31, accession 0000047217-25-000071, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 1.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. Secular decline in printing demand and supplies profitability

Printing supplies are a key profit and cash flow engine, and digitization, generic alternatives and the shift to big tank printers threaten long-term earnings and free cash flow that support dividends and debt service.

> decreased demand for printing products and solutions as a result of increased digitization, hybrid work, increasing competition from generic alternatives and customer preferences shifting from traditional disposable ink cartridge printers to refillable big tank printers.

## 2. Tariffs and trade restrictions raising costs not fully offset

With approximately 65% of net revenue from outside the United States and a concentrated Asian supply chain, sustained tariffs and retaliatory measures directly compress margins and cash flows, and mitigation is costly and slow.

> As a result, during fiscal 2025, we experienced higher commodity and tariff costs, which were not fully mitigated by pricing and other actions enacted during the period.

## 3. Rising memory and component costs squeezing Personal Systems margins

Personal Systems is a thin-margin business heavily reliant on third-party components and OMs, so input cost inflation that cannot be passed on to customers quickly erodes profitability.

> resulting from market dynamics, especially increasing memory and storage costs and potential supply chain constraints in our Personal Systems business, and higher labor and material costs borne by our manufacturers and suppliers that we are unable to pass on to our customers.

## 4. AI transition execution and cannibalization of traditional products

HP is investing significant resources in AI-enabled products whose returns are uncertain, and shifting demand to them may lower revenue or margins on its core non-AI portfolio.

> Moreover, our actions to drive demand to AI products may result in cannibalization of demand for our traditional, non-AI products, which may result in lower net revenues and/or profit margins.

## 5. Credit rating downgrade and debt refinancing risk

A downgrade would raise borrowing costs, limit commercial paper access and require collateral posting, which matters directly to lenders and could force cuts to buybacks or dividends.

> A downgrade of our current credit rating could increase the cost of borrowing under our credit facilities, reduce access to capital markets and/or market capacity for our commercial paper or require the posting of additional collateral under some of our derivative contracts.
