# Top 5 risks: Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2026-01-30, accession 0001571996-26-000008, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 2.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. AI server demand concentrated in a few large customers

AI is the main growth engine, but reliance on a small number of large buyers makes revenue lumpy and pressures margins. These deals also tie up working capital and extend credit, raising inventory and collection risk.

> to date our AI solutions have been purchased primarily by a small number of larger customers and cloud service providers. If we are not successful in continuing to expand sales to a broader base of customers, our ability to maintain growth in this area may be limited.

## 2. Large debt load with capacity for additional borrowing

About $31.5 billion of debt, plus room for up to $5.0 billion of commercial paper and $5.9 billion under the revolver, uses a large share of operating cash flow for debt service. Loose covenants on additional unsecured debt matter directly to lenders' recovery prospects.

> As of January 30, 2026, we and our subsidiaries had approximately $31.5 billion of indebtedness.

## 3. Supply chain dependence on concentrated vendors and contract manufacturers

Dell outsources all components and much of its assembly to a few Asian contract manufacturers, and it carries minimal inventory. Shortages, tariffs or price increases can therefore quickly hit revenue and gross margins.

> Because we generally maintain minimal levels of component and product inventories, a disruption in component or product availability could harm our ability to fill customer orders on a timely basis and at an acceptable price.

## 4. Structural pressure on the PC business (CSG)

CSG absorbs overhead and provides procurement scale, so falling PC demand or longer replacement cycles would weigh on consolidated operating income, not just on the segment itself.

> CSG faces risks and uncertainties from fundamental changes in the personal computer market, including a decline in worldwide revenues for notebooks, desktops, and workstations, and lower shipment forecasts for these products due to a general lengthening of the replacement cycle.

## 5. Controlling shareholders limit Class C holders' influence

Michael Dell and Silver Lake hold most of the voting power, so public shareholders have little say over directors, mergers or capital allocation. Their interests can diverge from those of minority holders, which may depress the valuation of the Class C stock.

> collectively held common stock representing approximately 91.7% of the total voting power of our outstanding common stock as of March 9, 2026.
