# Top 5 risks: Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2026-01-30, accession 0001571996-26-000008, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 1.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. AI revenue concentrated in a few large customers, straining working capital

AI is Dell's main growth driver, but a few large buyers and cloud providers account for most AI sales. That leaves revenue lumpy, margins under pricing pressure, and cash flow exposed to big component purchases, inventory build-ups and longer customer payment terms.

> to date our AI solutions have been purchased primarily by a small number of larger customers and cloud service providers. If we are not successful in continuing to expand sales to a broader base of customers, our ability to maintain growth in this area may be limited.

## 2. Supply shortages and rising component costs with minimal inventory buffer

Dell buys all of its components from outside vendors and relies on a few contract manufacturers in Asia, while holding very little inventory. Shortages, tariffs or price spikes therefore hit shipments and gross margin quickly, especially while AI demand is high.

> Because we generally maintain minimal levels of component and product inventories, a disruption in component or product availability could harm our ability to fill customer orders on a timely basis and at an acceptable price.

## 3. Large debt load limits financial flexibility

Debt service takes a substantial share of operating cash flow, leaving less for investment, dividends and buybacks. Dell can also add more debt through its commercial paper program and revolving credit facility, which matters directly to lenders.

> As of January 30, 2026, we and our subsidiaries had approximately $31.5 billion of indebtedness.

## 4. Structural decline and longer replacement cycles in the PC market

CSG, the PC business, absorbs Dell's overhead costs and gives it scale in purchasing. Shrinking PC revenue and slower replacement cycles could therefore hurt operating income across the whole company.

> CSG faces risks and uncertainties from fundamental changes in the personal computer market, including a decline in worldwide revenues for notebooks, desktops, and workstations, and lower shipment forecasts for these products due to a general lengthening of the replacement cycle.

## 5. Founder and Silver Lake control nearly all voting power

Class C shareholders have almost no influence over directors, mergers or capital allocation, and the controlling holders' interests may differ from theirs. The large number of convertible shares these holders could sell also weighs on the stock price.

> Michael Dell and the Susan Lieberman Dell Separate Property Trust (the "MD stockholders") and certain investment funds affiliated with Silver Lake Partners (the "SLP stockholders") collectively held common stock representing approximately 91.7% of the total voting power of our outstanding common stock as of March 9, 2026.
