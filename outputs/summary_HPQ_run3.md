# Top 5 risks: HP INC (HPQ)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2025-10-31, accession 0000047217-25-000071, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 3.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. Tariffs and trade restrictions squeezing costs and margins

HP's manufacturing and suppliers are concentrated in Asia, and the new tariffs have already raised costs by more than HP could recover through pricing. If the tariffs last, they directly cut operating income and cash flow.

> As a result, during fiscal 2025, we experienced higher commodity and tariff costs, which were not fully mitigated by pricing and other actions enacted during the period.

## 2. Long-term decline in the high-margin print and ink supplies business

Printing supplies have historically produced much of HP's profit and cash flow. Falling print demand and the shift to big tank printers, which bring few follow-on cartridge sales, put that long-term cash generation at risk.

> decreased demand for printing products and solutions as a result of increased digitization, hybrid work, increasing competition from generic alternatives and customer preferences shifting from traditional disposable ink cartridge printers to refillable big tank printers

## 3. Rising memory and component costs hurting PC gross margins

Personal Systems is a high-volume, thin-margin business. Rising memory and storage costs that HP cannot pass on to customers lower its gross margin and earnings.

> increases in component and manufacturing costs, resulting from market dynamics, especially increasing memory and storage costs and potential supply chain constraints in our Personal Systems business

## 4. Credit rating downgrade raising funding costs and limiting liquidity

For lenders and shareholders, a downgrade would raise borrowing costs, limit commercial paper access and require more collateral. Protecting the rating could also mean less cash for dividends and buybacks.

> A downgrade of our current credit rating could increase the cost of borrowing under our credit facilities, reduce access to capital markets and/or market capacity for our commercial paper or require the posting of additional collateral under some of our derivative contracts.

## 5. Heavy AI investment may not pay off and may cannibalize sales

HP is spending heavily on AI-enabled products, but those investments may not be recovered. Pushing customers toward AI products could also take sales from existing products and lower revenue or margins.

> Moreover, our actions to drive demand to AI products may result in cannibalization of demand for our traditional, non-AI products, which may result in lower net revenues and/or profit margins.
