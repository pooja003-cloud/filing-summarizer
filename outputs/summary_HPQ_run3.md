# Top 5 risks: HP INC (HPQ)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2025-10-31, accession 0000047217-25-000071, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 3.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. Tariffs and trade restrictions squeezing costs and margins

Tariff costs have already hit results and were not fully passed on to customers, so prolonged or escalating tariffs could keep compressing earnings and cash flow. HP's supply chain is concentrated in affected regions, which raises the exposure.

> As a result, during fiscal 2025, we experienced higher commodity and tariff costs, which were not fully mitigated by pricing and other actions enacted during the period.

## 2. Secular decline and mix shift eroding high-margin print supplies

High-margin ink cartridge sales are a key profit and cash flow engine. Digitization, generic supplies and the move to big tank printers threaten that model's long-term profitability.

> While our big tank printers typically have a higher initial margin than our traditional in cartridge printers, they do not result in follow-on sales of high margin traditional ink cartridges and, as a result, may have lower overall device lifetime profitability.

## 3. Rising memory and component costs compressing Personal Systems margins

PCs operate on thin margins, so component cost inflation that cannot be passed on to customers falls directly on operating profit. Securing supply early can also tie up working capital.

> gross margin declines in certain businesses, reflecting the effect of competitive pricing pressures and increases in component and manufacturing costs, resulting from market dynamics, especially increasing memory and storage costs and potential supply chain constraints in our Personal Systems business

## 4. AI transition execution and cannibalization of legacy products

HP is investing heavily in AI-enabled products, and failing to keep pace with competitors could cost market share and leave the investment unrecovered. Success could also cannibalize existing product sales and margins.

> Moreover, our actions to drive demand to AI products may result in cannibalization of demand for our traditional, non-AI products, which may result in lower net revenues and/or profit margins.

## 5. Credit rating downgrade raising borrowing costs and limiting funding

A downgrade would raise financing costs, limit commercial paper access and could trigger collateral calls, which matters directly to lenders. Defending the rating could also divert cash away from dividends and buybacks.

> A downgrade of our current credit rating could increase the cost of borrowing under our credit facilities, reduce access to capital markets and/or market capacity for our commercial paper or require the posting of additional collateral under some of our derivative contracts.
