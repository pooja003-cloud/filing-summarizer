# Top 5 risks: Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2026-01-30, accession 0001571996-26-000008, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 1.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. AI server demand concentrated among few large customers

Dell's AI growth depends on a small group of large buyers, which makes revenue lumpy and exposes margins to intense pricing pressure. Large orders also tie up working capital, add inventory risk and extend credit exposure, which affects both earnings quality and cash flow.

> to date our AI solutions have been purchased primarily by a small number of larger customers and cloud service providers. If we are not successful in continuing to expand sales to a broader base of customers, our ability to maintain growth in this area may be limited.

## 2. Component supply shortages and cost inflation from concentrated vendors

Dell sources all components from third parties, relies on a few contract manufacturers in Asia and holds minimal inventory. Shortages or price spikes can directly hit shipments and gross margins, especially during periods of rapid AI demand.

> Because we generally maintain minimal levels of component and product inventories, a disruption in component or product availability could harm our ability to fill customer orders on a timely basis and at an acceptable price.

## 3. Substantial indebtedness constrains cash flow and financial flexibility

A large debt load means a substantial portion of operating cash flow goes to debt service, reducing funds for investment, dividends and buybacks. Debt terms generally do not restrict additional unsecured borrowing, which matters to lenders assessing leverage and subordination risk.

> As of January 30, 2026, we and our subsidiaries had approximately $31.5 billion of indebtedness.

## 4. Tariffs, trade disputes and adverse macroeconomic conditions

With global manufacturing and significant non-U.S. sales, tariffs and trade restrictions can raise costs, depress demand and disrupt supply chains. Macro weakness can also delay customer IT spending, pressuring both revenue and profitability.

> Trade policies and disputes could result in increased tariffs and other trade restrictions and protectionist measures, which could increase our manufacturing costs, increase prices of and reduce demand for our products

## 5. Controlling stockholders dominate voting through multi-class share structure

Michael Dell and Silver Lake control nearly all voting power, so Class C holders have little influence over directors, mergers or capital allocation. Their interests may conflict with those of minority shareholders and creditors, and the structure may weigh on the valuation.

> collectively held common stock representing approximately 91.7% of the total voting power of our outstanding common stock as of March 9, 2026.
