# Top 5 risks: HP INC (HPQ)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2025-10-31, accession 0000047217-25-000071, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 2.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. Secular decline in printing demand and supplies profitability

Printing, especially high-margin ink cartridge supplies, is a key profit and cash flow engine. Digitization, hybrid work, generic supplies and the shift to big tank printers erode recurring supplies revenue and lifetime device profitability.

> decreased demand for printing products and solutions as a result of increased digitization, hybrid work, increasing competition from generic alternatives and customer preferences shifting from traditional disposable ink cartridge printers to refillable big tank printers.

## 2. Tariffs and trade restrictions raising costs and squeezing margins

New, substantial U.S. tariffs and retaliatory measures have already raised costs that pricing actions did not fully offset. Sustained tariffs could significantly hurt results of operations and cash flows while costly supply chain shifts take time to deliver benefits.

> As a result, during fiscal 2025, we experienced higher commodity and tariff costs, which were not fully mitigated by pricing and other actions enacted during the period.

## 3. Rising memory, storage and component costs in Personal Systems

Personal Systems runs on thin margins and depends heavily on outsourced manufacturers and single-source processor suppliers. Inflation in memory and storage costs that cannot be passed through directly compresses gross margin and cash generation.

> increases in component and manufacturing costs, resulting from market dynamics, especially increasing memory and storage costs and potential supply chain constraints in our Personal Systems business, and higher labor and material costs borne by our manufacturers and suppliers that we are unable to pass on to our customers.

## 4. Aggressive price competition and market share losses

HP has already faced market share declines and has had to cut prices to stay competitive, which pressures revenue and gross margin. Lower-cost rivals and yen weakness benefiting Japanese print competitors make this pressure persistent.

> We have faced and expect to continue to face aggressive price competition and have lowered and may in the future need to lower the prices of many of our products and services to stay competitive

## 5. Credit rating downgrade and debt refinancing risk

A downgrade would raise borrowing costs, restrict commercial paper access and require more collateral. It could also force debt reduction that limits dividends and buybacks, and would hurt the subscription businesses that depend on financing.

> A downgrade of our current credit rating could increase the cost of borrowing under our credit facilities, reduce access to capital markets and/or market capacity for our commercial paper or require the posting of additional collateral under some of our derivative contracts.
