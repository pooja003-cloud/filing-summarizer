# Top 5 risks: Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL)

Source: Form 10-K, fiscal year ended 2026-01-30, accession 0001571996-26-000008, Item 1A Risk Factors. Run 3.

Quotes are unverified until check.py has run.

## 1. AI demand concentrated in few large customers, straining margins and cash

AI servers are Dell's main growth driver, but lumpy orders from a few large buyers bring pricing pressure, working-capital demands, inventory risk and customer credit exposure. Earnings and cash flow could swing sharply from period to period.

> to date our AI solutions have been purchased primarily by a small number of larger customers and cloud service providers. If we are not successful in continuing to expand sales to a broader base of customers, our ability to maintain growth in this area may be limited.

## 2. Component shortages, cost inflation and reliance on concentrated suppliers

Dell holds minimal inventory and depends on a few contract manufacturers and single-source suppliers, mostly in Asia. A component shortage or price spike would directly hit revenue and gross margins.

> Because we generally maintain minimal levels of component and product inventories, a disruption in component or product availability could harm our ability to fill customer orders on a timely basis and at an acceptable price.

## 3. Substantial indebtedness and capacity to borrow more

About $31.5 billion of debt uses a large share of operating cash flow for debt service. Loose covenants allow more borrowing, which matters for both lenders' recovery and equity holders' flexibility.

> As of January 30, 2026, we and our subsidiaries had approximately $31.5 billion of indebtedness.

## 4. Tariffs, trade disputes and protectionism raising costs and limiting sales

With Asia-based manufacturing and about 45% of revenue from outside the U.S., tariffs and trade restrictions could raise costs, reduce demand and block sales to certain customers.

> Trade policies and disputes could result in increased tariffs and other trade restrictions and protectionist measures, which could increase our manufacturing costs, increase prices of and reduce demand for our products, limit our ability to sell to certain customers

## 5. Structural PC market decline pressuring CSG revenue and overhead absorption

CSG revenue absorbs Dell's overhead and provides scaled procurement. Longer PC replacement cycles and falling PC revenues therefore weigh on consolidated profitability, not only on CSG.

> CSG faces risks and uncertainties from fundamental changes in the personal computer market, including a decline in worldwide revenues for notebooks, desktops, and workstations, and lower shipment forecasts for these products due to a general lengthening of the replacement cycle.
